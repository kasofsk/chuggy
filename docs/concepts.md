# Concept registry

A **routing table**, not a glossary. Each row maps a concept to the one doc that defines it and the heading that holds the definition. The definition itself stays where it is argued, because a definition divorced from its argument is worth less — extracted into an alphabetical list it becomes a sentence to memorize.

This page holds no definitions of its own.

## What the registry is for

`docs.md` states the rule: a concept is defined once and mentioned freely. This file is what makes that rule checkable — the gate looks only at **registered** terms, so a row is what turns enforcement on for a concept.

Two consequences worth understanding before adding a row:

**The cost of a wrong row is paid by whoever writes it.** An unregistered term is invisible to the gate however it is written; a registered one commits every other doc in the tree.

**The owner is exempt because its row names it**, not because a list of exemptions says so.

## The criterion for a row

Both halves, or no row:

1. a reader must understand the term to read some *other* doc, **and**
2. more than one doc explains it.

The second half is what keeps this a routing table. A term explained in exactly one place needs no routing — it needs a link.

Before adding a row, check the term has exactly **one** definition today. A row over two definitions fails the gate on merge, and the temptation then is to weaken the rule rather than fix the duplicate.

The registry is meant to stay around a dozen rows. A hundred rows would be the glossary this design rejected, reached by a different route.

## The registry

*Empty by design.* No term has met both halves of the criterion yet: where one doc needs a term another owns, it links to the owner rather than re-explaining it. Rows arrive as the corpus does.
